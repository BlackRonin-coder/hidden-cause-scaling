# Hidden Causes — Public Research Overview

> **Purpose:** Present a falsifiable way of thinking about unobserved causes through measured effects. This is a public overview, not a complete physical model or a new discovery claim.

## Where the idea began

Many scientific questions involve entities or mechanisms that cannot be photographed or inspected directly. Instead, they are examined through measurable effects: orbital deviations, differences in light, instrument signals or unexpected system behaviour.

Dark matter was one motivating example: gravitational phenomena can expose a mismatch between a simplified model of visible matter and observations. That mismatch motivates testing—but it does not identify the microscopic source.

The same distinction appears in systems engineering. A deviation tells us where understanding is incomplete. It does not, by itself, tell us which component failed.

## The publicly shared reasoning

The essential research question can be expressed without assuming any particular hidden entity:

1. **State a baseline.** What would the system do if the currently understood causes were sufficient?
2. **Measure an observation.** What actually happened? Include measurement and modelling uncertainty.
3. **Audit the difference.** Is the apparent difference an artefact of calibration, selection, noise, background or a mistaken baseline?
4. **Test repeatability.** Does the structure survive another observation or a change in measurement conditions?
5. **Challenge the explanation.** What ordinary explanations could produce the same appearance? Which observations would distinguish them?
6. **Withhold evidence.** Specify a prediction before inspecting new measurements.
7. **Revise or reject.** Evidence must be able to defeat a favoured explanation.

These are established scientific practices. The contribution offered publicly is a **personal synthesis of the investigative sequence**, not a claim to have invented residual analysis, Bayesian inference or indirect detection.

## One pattern, different mechanisms

| Context | Observable consequence | Crucial caution |
|---|---|---|
| Exoplanets | Changes in starlight or stellar motion | Other astrophysical or instrument effects can imitate a signal. |
| Crystallography | Diffraction and reconstructed density | A reconstruction depends on measurement and modelling assumptions. |
| Galactic dynamics | Rotation and gravitating-mass inference | Visible-matter modelling and alternative dynamics need careful treatment. |
| Gravitational lensing | Deflection of light and inferred mass distribution | Geometry and other modelling choices matter. |
| General systems engineering | Difference between expected and measured behaviour | A deviation alone does not identify its root cause. |

The *reasoning pattern* can recur across domains. **The physical response mechanisms are not interchangeable.** A method should not jump from one scale to another by analogy alone.

## What would advance the research?

Credible next steps include a narrowly defined public dataset, a documented baseline, independent predictions, explicit comparison models, transparent failure criteria and informed scientific review.

The toy code in this repository illustrates a simpler introductory concept. It does **not** implement a dark-matter analysis or establish any astrophysical result.
