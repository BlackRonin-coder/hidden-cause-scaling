# Scientific Boundaries and Falsification

This repository separates an **investigative perspective**, a **synthetic illustration** and an **independently tested scientific result**.

## What is supported here

- It is legitimate to infer possible hidden causes from measurable effects, provided alternatives and uncertainties are tested.
- Residuals are observations relative to an assumed model, not self-interpreting facts.
- Multiple genuinely independent measurements can help constrain explanations.
- New predictions and null results are essential tests of a candidate explanation.
- A small synthetic example can demonstrate this logic without providing evidence about nature.

## What is **not** claimed

- Discovery or experimental confirmation of a dark-matter particle, field or detector.
- An independently verified new law of physics.
- A validated theory of quantum gravity.
- That known astrophysical residuals can only be explained by one proposed mechanism.
- That a numerical toy example validates a real-world dark-matter hypothesis.
- That the broader underlying mathematical research has been fully disclosed or independently accepted.

## Common failure modes

| Tempting claim | More defensible treatment |
|---|---|
| “A residual proves a hidden object exists.” | A residual may reflect model error, noise, bias or an unmodelled ordinary process. |
| “Two anomalies must share a cause.” | Independently test whether a compatible explanation genuinely predicts both. |
| “An apparent good fit proves the theory.” | Use withheld data, error models, alternatives and sensitivity analysis. |
| “Null results can be ignored.” | Record what they rule out and narrow the tested explanation. |
| “The same pattern means the same physics.” | Similar inference structures do not imply identical mechanisms. |

## A falsification-oriented review checklist

1. Are the observations and units described precisely?
2. Are measurement uncertainty, selection effects and relevant background represented?
3. Can a better ordinary model remove the reported residual?
4. Is a candidate mechanism separately stated, rather than inferred from the residual alone?
5. Is the claim testable on measurements not used to formulate it?
6. What result would count *against* the explanation?
7. Does the conclusion distinguish evidence of a discrepancy from identification of a physical cause?

A sound research programme needs a route to being wrong. This document invites those questions.
